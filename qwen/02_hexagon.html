Field notes
on a spinning wall

Naïve reflection (v = v - 2(v·n)n) conserves energy it has no right to conserve. A rotating wall is a moving reference frame — treat it like one and the simulation becomes stable, sticky, and strange.

01

Signed distance to an edge

Each of the six edges gives an inward half-plane. With vertices A→B and inward normal n, a body is safe while d = (P−A)·n ≥ r. Penetration is pushed straight back along n.

n = normalize(center − midpoint(A,B))
02

Wall velocity

A point on the wall at radius q − c moves at ω × r. The body's velocity relative to the wall is what gets reflected.

v_wall = ω · (−(q.y−c.y), (q.x−c.x))
03

Impulse, then friction

Normal component reverses with restitution e; tangential component is dragged toward the wall's tangential motion, capped by μ|jₙ|. That cap is what makes the hexagon stir the pile.

jₙ = −(1+e)(u·n), |jₜ| ≤ μ|jₙ|
04

Fixed substeps

Integration runs at a hard 240 Hz with an accumulator, two collision iterations per substep, and a speed ceiling. At 2600 px/s a body advances ~11 px per substep — less than its radius, so tunnelling stays impossible.

dt = 1/240, iterations = 2, v_max = 2600

Reflection in the wall's frame

Subtract v_wall, reflect the remainder about n, shear the tangential part toward the wall's motion, then add v_wall back. The body leaves with the wall's momentum folded in — the chamber pumps energy upward until gravity and drag find an equilibrium that visibly breathes.
